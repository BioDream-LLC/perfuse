Exchange

Reporting, national exchange, documents, apps and images

Beyond the hospital's own interfaces, data has to reach public health agencies, other organisations across the country, patients' apps and imaging archives. Each has its own rules. Perfuse follows them.

Public health reporting

Reports that go because a code matched

What it is

Electronic case reporting (eCR) tells the health department about a reportable condition. Electronic lab reporting (ELR) sends it the lab results it must see.

Why it exists

Outbreaks are found from these reports. Sent by hand, they arrive late or not at all.

How you use it

Load the trigger code list, add an eCR or ELR destination to a channel, and only the messages that should be reported are sent. The agency's answer is stored with the report.

  • HL7 eCR 2.1.2 eICR documents, validated with the HL7 validator
  • Reportability Responses received and stored where the report said to look
  • ELR 2.5.1: ONC's ELR certification tool reports all 28 of its example messages valid

TEFCA and UDAP

Records from organisations you have never connected to

What it is

TEFCA is the US national framework for exchanging health records. UDAP is how two systems that have never met prove who they are, with certificates from a trust community.

Why it exists

A patient treated in another state should not have to carry their records by hand.

How you use it

Register with your network's trust community, give Perfuse its certificate, and request or answer Facilitated FHIR exchanges. Every exchange is written to disk for audit.

Guide: TEFCA and UDAP explained

SMART on FHIR

Let apps in, safely

What it is

SMART on FHIR is how an app asks a patient or clinician to sign in and gets only the access they agree to.

Why it exists

CMS requires patient apps to be able to reach health plan data. Each app must get exactly the access granted, and no more.

How you use it

Register the app in the console under Users, SMART apps. Perfuse is the sign-in server and the data server, so there is nothing else to run.

  • Standalone and EHR launch, PKCE, consent, refresh tokens and Backend Services
  • Granular scopes enforced, for example only lab results
  • Inferno SMART App Launch 2.2: 80 of 80
Guide: SMART on FHIR explained

Imaging

Images routed, and stripped of names where they must be

What it is

DICOM is the standard for medical images and the network services that move them between scanners and archives.

Why it exists

Images go to many places: the archive, a specialist, a research team. Some must never carry the patient's name.

How you use it

Make a channel with a DICOM source, add a de-identification step, and send to one or more archives or onward as HL7 or FHIR.

  • C-STORE, C-FIND, C-ECHO, C-MOVE and C-GET, in both directions
  • DICOMweb: an archive served over QIDO-RS, WADO-RS and STOW-RS, browsable in the OHIF viewer
  • Tested against the Orthanc archive, checked in Orthanc's own catalogue, DICOMweb both ways
Guide: DICOM routing and de-identification

Document sharing and Direct

Clinical documents to the organisations that need them

What it is

IHE XDS.b and XCA are how hospitals and health information exchanges register, find and fetch clinical documents, as Carequality and eHealth Exchange do. Direct is secure, signed and encrypted email between providers.

Why it exists

Referrals, discharge summaries and records requests still move as documents, and most of them still go by fax.

How you use it

Turn on the registry and repository, or point an XDS destination at your exchange. For Direct, give Perfuse its address and certificate, then send and receive like any channel.

  • XDS.b registry and repository (ITI-41, ITI-18, ITI-43), an XCA gateway (ITI-38, ITI-39), MHD, PIXm and PDQm on FHIR
  • Tested against the NIST XDS Toolkit
  • Direct: S/MIME signed and encrypted messages, XDM packages, MDN receipts, certificates from DNS; checked against OpenSSL in both directions
  • A CMS Aligned Network screen: each of the 26 framework criteria and where this server stands

Master patient index

One person, however many systems know them

What it is

An index of the patients every system sends, linking each person's records under one enterprise identifier, with the reasons each link was made.

Why it exists

The lab, the clinic and the hospital each give the same person their own number, and a record that cannot be matched is a history nobody sees.

How you use it

Turn it on, add a patient index destination to the channels carrying registrations, and review the possible matches it queues. Other systems ask it through FHIR Patient/$match and PIXm.

  • Trusted identifiers link at once; everything else is scored (Fellegi-Sunter weights, Jaro-Winkler names) and waits for a person, unless an administrator lets the certain ones link
  • Weights estimated from the site's own records
  • Measured on the FEBRL test data: no record linked to the wrong person
  • A40 merges, links and unlinks kept with their history; built on main, in the next release

Immunization registries and patient summaries

Vaccines reported, histories fetched, summaries on demand

What it is

State immunization registries take vaccine reports over the CDC's IIS web service. The International Patient Summary is a short, standard record of a patient's problems, medicines, allergies and vaccines.

Why it exists

Every vaccine given must reach the state registry, and a patient seen somewhere new should arrive with a summary, not a stack of paper.

How you use it

Add an immunization registry destination with your state's preset. Ask the FHIR server for Patient/$summary, or share one with the patient through a Health link.

  • CDC IIS SOAP web service as source and destination; VXU submissions with ACK handling; QBP/RSP history queries
  • IPS Bundles from the FHIR store, shared as SMART Health Links
