Connectors

Every way in, every way out

A connector is how Perfuse reaches another system: the cable, the folder, the queue or the web address messages travel through. Perfuse has 24 kinds of source and 26 kinds of destination, and the healthcare formats that travel over them.

Healthcare formats

The languages health systems speak

A format is the shape of the message itself. Hospitals use several at once, often for the same patient: the admission in HL7 v2, the record in FHIR, the X-ray in DICOM, the bill in X12. Perfuse reads, checks, writes and converts all of them.

ICON:doc

HL7 v2

The workhorse of hospital interfaces since the 1990s: admissions, orders, results, scheduling. Parsed fast, every quirk handled, acknowledgements generated.

ICON:layers

FHIR R4, R4B and R5

The modern web standard for health data. A full FHIR server, a validator, and conversion from HL7 v2 to FHIR and US Core.

ICON:image

DICOM

Medical images. All five network services, sending and receiving, with de-identification as a step in the channel.

ICON:payer

X12

Insurance transactions: 837 claims, 835 remittances, 270/271 eligibility, 278 prior authorization and 275 attachments.

ICON:book

CDA and C-CDA

Clinical documents, including those hidden inside HL7 v2 messages. Checked, signed, repaired and rendered to PDF.

ICON:flask

NCPDP, JSON, XML and delimited

Pharmacy messages, and the general formats partners invent for themselves.

The classic hospital connections

Most interfaces in a hospital still travel over a few well-worn routes. Perfuse speaks each in both directions, with encryption where the route allows it.

Cloud and message queues

Straight into the cloud, without the cloud's toolkits

Perfuse talks to AWS, Azure and Google Cloud over their own web interfaces, with the signing done inside Perfuse, so nothing extra is installed. A message taken from a queue is confirmed only once it has been handled, so a crash means it is read again, never lost.
